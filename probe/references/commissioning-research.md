# Commissioning a research pass

For when a probe outgrows a one-line derivation and becomes a dispatched research pass (deep
research, a literature sweep, multiple agents against the same codebase question).

**Angles buy coverage; only redundancy buys error-correction.** Passes given deliberately
different angles find zero head-on contradictions between them by construction — different
angles look at different things, not the same thing twice. If you need error-correction, not just
breadth, point two passes at the *same* question and compare their answers directly.

**The auditor is the valuable half.** The audit layer catches what the research passes themselves
miss: a nonexistent paper cited as the strongest evidence, a welded citation, a misread primary
source repeated across passes, a double-count of correlated evidence, a theory the design's own
argument depends on invalidated by its own assumptions. Budget the audit pass as load-bearing, not
a formality after the "real" work.

**A pass under-ranks its own best finding.** Its prioritization is unreliable — it can bury the
most consequential finding as a throwaway aside while promoting a cheap add-on to centerpiece.
Re-rank the findings yourself against what actually threatens or supports the premise under test.

This holds for **any** commissioned pass, adversarial review included, and your re-ranking is
where you are most likely to err. Two ways it goes wrong, both observed here: ranking a finding by
how often its surface fires rather than by the damage one firing does, and deferring a finding on
an unchecked premise about the situation — "that collision is rare" — that you found convenient.
Rank by damage per occurrence, and treat a premise that justifies deferring work as exactly the
kind that earns a probe.

**A pass that omits a finding cutting against its own recommendation is arguing, not reporting.**
Require every pass to state explicitly what it found that argues against the conclusion it is
proposing, even if it still recommends that conclusion.

**Primary sources beat secondary by an order of magnitude.** A secondary source's only durable
contribution is usually a pointer back to a primary one. Treat a secondary-only sweep of a topic
as one orientation pass, never as the evidence base itself.
